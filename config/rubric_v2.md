# Einstein Moving — Sales Call Rubric v2 (Jeff Johnson / Northwood Call Map)

**Effective:** rollout run of 2026-09-08.
**Supersedes:** `rubric.md` (the old 85-pt scorecard) as the primary weekly grading rubric. `rubric.md` stays in the repo unchanged — it's the source for every rep's pre-training baseline score (see **Baseline handling** below), not deleted.
**Total:** 100 pts. **Passing / Green: 80+.**
**Source documents:** Northwood Group "Call Map" (9/1/26), Einstein Call Sheet (9/1/26), Sales Script Framework (rev. 8/18/26).

---

## Overview — The Call Map

3 stages, 9 checkpoints:

- **STAGE 1 — Discovery** *(earn trust)*: 1 Quid pro quo → 2 Their agenda → 3 Two layers deep → 4 "Careful with" (Sheet 1) → 5 "Who's coming" (Sheet 2)
- **STAGE 2 — Ramp up, take away**: 6 The takeaway (Sheets 3–4)
- **STAGE 3 — After the estimate**: 7 The estimate → 8 Save Your Ass (Sheet 5) → 9 Close

**Critical rule — no differentiator said twice.** Sheets 1–4 (damage coverage, Meet Your Mover, On-Time Guarantee, Communication) can land in Stage 1 *(as a direct answer to a concern the customer raised)* **or** Stage 2 *(as the "takeaway" ask, for whatever wasn't already covered)* — never both, never neither. Grade for "was it covered, in a valid slot," not "was it said in the Stage 2 slot specifically." Save Your Ass (Sheet 5) is NOT part of this either/or — it always belongs in Stage 3, after the estimate, no matter what happened earlier.

## Section-by-section (100 pts)

### Stage 1 — Discovery (40 pts)

**1. Quid pro quo (5 pts)**
Rep trades the ask for a benefit before probing — a version of: *"Let's get you quoted / let's start with a few questions to get us on the right track, should take about six minutes, then we'll build you an accurate quote."* Paraphrase counts.
- 5 pts: framing present (time-boxed ask + promise of an accurate quote in return)
- 2 pts: rep asks questions but skips the framing entirely
- 0 pts: launches straight into probing, no framing at all

**2. Their agenda (10 pts)** — the single highest-priority line in the script.
Recognizable version of: *"Other than price, what are your biggest concerns about this move, and what do you want answers to on this call?"*
- 10 pts: asked, and asked **before** logistics probing starts
- 4 pts: asked, but after probing has already started
- 0 pts: never asked

**3. Two layers deep (15 pts)** — heaviest single item in Stage 1. Matches the CET agenda-opener finding that "asked the line but did nothing with it" is the dominant failure mode.
For each concern raised, did the rep go at least two layers deep — *"tell me more about that," "what else is on your list"* — continuing until the customer says some version of "that covers it," rather than one question and a pivot?
- 15 pts: real two-layer follow-up on all concerns raised (typically 2–3)
- 8 pts: attempted but shallow (one question, no real second layer), or done for only some concerns
- 0 pts: concern acknowledged with no follow-up, straight pivot to logistics/pricing
- **15 pts (N/A/full credit) if the customer never raised any concern at all** — i.e., item #2 either wasn't asked, or was asked and drew nothing to dig into. A follow-up gap can't exist if there was never anything to follow up on; that is not the rep's failure to score against. (This is an explicit exception to the general N/A rule below, not an omission — item #3 IS eligible for N/A, unlike items #4/#5/#6.)

**4. "Careful with" — Sheet 1 / damage coverage (5 pts)**
*"What items in this move do you need us to be most careful with?"* → the "we move it like it's ours" / up to $2,000 coverage answer.
- 5 pts if covered anywhere in the call in a valid slot: either as a direct answer in Stage 1 (customer raised a damage/carefulness concern, rep answered it there) or as part of the Stage 2 takeaway if it wasn't raised in Stage 1.
- 0 pts if never covered anywhere.

**5. "Who's coming" — Sheet 2 / Meet Your Mover (5 pts)**
Same logic as #4, for the "who's actually showing up" concern / Meet Your Mover answer — credit if covered anywhere in a valid slot, regardless of which stage it landed in.

### Stage 2 — Ramp up, take away (15 pts)

**6. The takeaway — Sheets 3 & 4, On-Time + Communication (15 pts)**
The "ramp-up" line — naming what the customer *hasn't* brought up (on-time performance, communication) and using that to earn the ask, then delivering both.
- 15 pts: On-Time Guarantee **and** Communication (day-before call, named crew lead, inbox updates) both land somewhere in the call — either answered organically in Stage 1 (if the customer raised them) or delivered here as the takeaway.
- −7.5 pts for each of the two (On-Time / Communication) that's never covered anywhere in the call.
- A repeat (something already covered in Stage 1, said again here) is not penalized on this line directly — that's caught by Process Discipline below.

### Stage 3 — After the estimate (35 pts)

**7. The estimate — anchor then adjust (10 pts)**
*"A typical [size] takes [N] movers [X–Y] hours. Since you're [floor/walk/boxes/drive], I'd expect you toward the [top/middle] of that."*
- 10 pts: gives a typical range **and** personalizes it to something specific the customer mentioned
- 5 pts: gives a range but doesn't personalize
- 0 pts: no anchor/range framing at all

**8. Save Your Ass — Sheet 5 (15 pts)**, 5 lines, 3 pts each:
- "We commit to the rate" / experience framing (100k moves)
- "The estimate is a window because your move is yours" (specific factors named)
- "If the crew beats it, you pay less" (15-min increments past the minimum)
- The self-prep tip ("anything you get done before we show up comes off your bill")
- "Does that feel fair?" — asked, **and** followed by silence (no immediate re-pitch)

**9. Close (5 pts)**
Rate-lock line ("your rate locks when you book") + explicit ask ("would you like our help with your move?" or equivalent). 2.5 pts each, rounded to nearest whole point at the total-score level.

### Cross-call scoring (10 + 5 pts)

**Rapport/warmth (10 pts)** — same 1–5 transcript-based scale as the old rubric's phone-presence assessment (unchanged criteria, just rescaled ×2 to fit the 100-pt total: score × 2). Same audio-review-flag rules carry over.

**Process discipline (5 pts)** — nothing said twice (a differentiator repeated across Stage 1 and Stage 2), and silence actually held after each open-ended question (the agenda question, "does that make sense," "does that feel fair," the close). Deduct 1 pt per violation, floor at 0.

## N/A rule

Same convention as the old rubric: if a bullet's underlying situation doesn't apply to this call (e.g., no packing discussed, so packing-specific lines can't be graded), automatic full credit — don't penalize for a scenario that never came up.

**This does not apply to items #4, #5, #6** — those have their own explicit either/or logic above (Stage 1 vs. Stage 2 credit). They are never simply "N/A"; one of the two slots always owns the score. **Item #3 is the one explicit exception** — see its own N/A clause above (no concern raised = full credit).

## Walkthrough-scheduler calls (resolved 2026-09-07)

Some intake calls correctly route to a virtual walkthrough before any pricing happens — large moves (>=1800 sq ft) where a full quote isn't given on this call by design, not because the rep failed to reach it. Mark these `call_type: "walkthrough_scheduler"`.

**Scoring rule:** if the call properly and cleanly hands off to a scheduled walkthrough (the rep didn't abandon the call mid-pitch, didn't get cut off, actually confirmed next steps) — score Stage 3 items (#7 estimate, #8 Save Your Ass, #9 close) as **N/A/full credit** (10 + 15 + 5 = 30 pts), since those steps were never supposed to happen on this call. Stage 1 and Stage 2 items still score normally — discovery, the differentiators, and rapport all still apply and still matter on a walkthrough-routed call.

**Do NOT apply this N/A treatment** if the call just trails off, gets transferred without a clean handoff, or abandons the pitch mid-stream for reasons other than a genuine walkthrough routing — that's a real 0, not a deferred credit. The distinction is "correctly routed by design" vs. "didn't get there."

This replaces the former "open decision" of not having a walkthrough mini-rubric — a full separate rubric isn't built, but this scoring treatment is now the settled rule, not a per-grader judgment call.

## Baseline handling

Every rep's most recent score under the OLD 85-pt rubric (`rubric.md`) is locked in as their pre-training baseline. Starting with the rollout run, all grading uses this v2 rubric; going forward, reps are compared against their own v2 trajectory (beat-your-own-baseline), not against the old 85-pt number — the two scales don't convert to each other.
